The plain mannequins are placeholders for people; paint them as Florentine cavalry at the battle of Campaldino, 1289, each in exactly its place, size and direction:
the orange mannequin is Dante, 24, beardless, in a mail hauberk with a white surcoat bearing a small red lily, an open iron helmet so his face is visible, right arm raised, shouting;
the pale mannequins are Florentine knights in mail and white surcoats, simple iron helmets; the dark grey-green ones in the distance are the enemy riders of Arezzo in darker surcoats.
The horses are at full gallop: legs stretched and gathered, manes and tails flying, clods of earth and dust thrown up behind them, the banners with the red Florentine lily streaming. Everything leans forward into the charge.
