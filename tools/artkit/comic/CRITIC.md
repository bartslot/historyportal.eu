# The critic: grade the Blender composition BEFORE painting

Bart (2026-09-27): "you have to create a role that is critical, and grade the composition in blender first -
if you think it's comic book worthy then go." "Go for a 10." The director is Bart; the critic protects his time.

Look at `<shot>_shadedfig.png` (and `_clay.png`) and score each line 0-2. Paint only at 8+ of 10 (aim for 10).

1. **One focal point.** The eye lands on one thing first (the speaking character, the bright gap), placed on a
   third or framed; nothing else competes in contrast or size.
2. **Depth in three planes.** Foreground element (framing, overlapping, bigger), middle (the action),
   background (lighter, simpler). Nothing flat-on, no empty foreground band.
3. **Bodies tell the beat.** Poses read in silhouette: who speaks, who reacts, where they look. Hands and
   heads point to the story. No A-pose, no two figures in the same pose unless it is the point.
4. **Grounded, lit.** Feet and hooves touch the ground with contact shadows; one clear light direction;
   shade readable (never near-black: it gets painted as holes).
5. **Movement and variety.** Crowds and animals differ in heading, spacing, pose and size; diagonals over
   horizontals in action shots; nothing copy-pasted.

Then ask JEV (text) for every *content* choice in the shot (props, costume, banners, landscape): plausible or not,
keep or cut. For dramatic staging, plausibility is enough; it need not be documented (Bart: "shots like these don't
have to be historically correct as it's plausible it happened").
