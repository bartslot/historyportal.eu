HISTORY PORTAL — REUSABLE SCENE KITS

Art direction decision

Prioritise places where lessons happen: woodland, domestic room, working street, settlement yard. Existing libraries already cover skies, sea and outdoor props well. More isolated objects add less teaching range than coherent environments.

First batch: 12 sheets, 22 assets across two existing palette bundles. Five sheets complete nl-prehistory woodland; seven complete a modest nl-golden-age room. No new skies. No characters. No assets for unconfigured palettes.

Ranking method: each count below names distinct lesson scene setups a kit could support with existing assets and separate characters. Counts are editorial estimates, not measured usage or additive totals. Rankings apply within each bundle. Later kits remain proposals, outside batch1.json.

Shared construction rules

Exactly one full sky painting, one seamless top-down ground and one non-tiling far backdrop per scene, including interiors. Bands are standing screens, never extra grounds or extra backdrops. Layer kind describes an asset's role; placement depth remains independent. A mid wall fixture can sit beside a band at fg depth.

Camera stays level, eye height 1.6 m. Use frontal walls and shallow set-piece views; no painted vanishing-point room, wraparound corners or side-facing camera. Restrict camera movement to each composition's checked safe frame.

One hand throughout: existing ink drawing plus transparent watercolour, existing bundle palette, existing fixed two-pass pipeline. Pass-one and pass-two details stay unchanged. Reuse matching line weight, wash softness, material colour and lighting across each kit. Ground and bg remain full surfaces; loose objects have transparent surrounds. Watercolour translucency must not create accidental see-through holes in occluding walls or dense woodland screens.

Sheets contain one to six matching assets, independently separable with generous gutters. Ground: exactly one tile, seamless on both axes, no perspective or large baked shadows. Band: one to three wide strips stacked, each seamless sideways. Backdrop: one continuous panorama, never tiled. Subjects specify intended widths because height alone cannot preserve strip and floor scale. Ground height_m is 0: floor surface datum, not tile footprint. All other height_m values mean physical full asset height, not intended pixel height or placement elevation.

1. MISSING KITS, RANKED PER BUNDLE

NL-PREHISTORY — Funnelbeaker, northern Netherlands, c. 3400 BCE

1) Mixed woodland and clearing kit — 8 setups: gathering, wood selection, clearance, field edge, grazing edge, route through woodland, settlement boundary, approach to a burial monument.
Missing: forest-floor ground; non-tiling woodland backdrop; tiling trunk/canopy screen and hazel edge; woodland-shaped oak, lime and birch cut-outs; low undergrowth. Existing isolated trees remain useful, but do not supply layered forest enclosure. Add these pieces together, not another lone oak. FIRST BATCH: complete five-sheet kit.
Period treatment: mixed deciduous woodland, small clearings and cultivated margins. Avoid modern forestry rows, continuous spruce forest and a huge treeless agricultural plain. Keep dry woodland and wet alder/reed settings distinct.

2) House interior kit — 6 setups: grinding grain, preparing food, pottery use, tool maintenance, household storage, shelter around a hearth.
Missing: plain wattle-and-daub back wall band with structural posts; packed-earth floor tile; closed timber entrance panel and opaque dark entrance inset; simple hearth, low work surface, baskets/storage and bedding. Use existing pots, quern, bowls, flint and fire pit where scale fits. No glass windows, brick chimney, tiled floor, modern furniture or rows of formal window openings. Treat roof vent, furnishing and door details as reconstruction choices requiring a selected archaeological reference, not known facts.

3) Small settlement and working yard kit — 5 setups: house building, livestock care, harvest handling, communal food preparation, exchange between households.
Missing: bare trampled-earth ground; low boundary band; compatible TRB house variants/end elevation; simple animal enclosure and open work shelter. Reuse existing TRB house, fence, animals and tools. Keep settlement small and irregular. Existing LBK longhouse belongs to an earlier cultural context; exclude it from c. 3400 BCE TRB scenes. Ice-sheet backdrop also excluded from this setting.

4) Cultivated margin kit — 4 setups: sowing, weeding, grain harvest, grain processing outdoors.
Missing: tiling low crop band, stubble strip, cleared-soil ground and small harvested bundles. Reuse wheat patch, digging stick and quern. Keep field scale modest; no medieval strip-field geometry, steel plough or windmill.

NL-GOLDEN-AGE — Dutch town, c. 1640

1) Modest urban room kit — 8 setups: meal preparation, household meal, domestic work, reading, teaching, keeping accounts, inspecting trade samples, small commercial discussion.
Missing: tiling limewashed back wall; small-paver terracotta ground; leaded window, closed oak door, modest hooded fireplace; table, storage, chair, stool and bench. FIRST BATCH: complete seven-sheet kit. Existing charts, cloth, Delft jar, pewter jug, bucket, lantern and sea chest provide scene-specific dressing.
Keep room modest and flexible. No palace fireplace, plate-glass window or later stove. Work and domestic life can share a room; these scenes need not imply separate modern kitchens, offices and classrooms. Fireplace supplied unlit; lit variant comes later. Window supplied with opaque painted daylight inset, not an actual opening in the wall.

2) Street and frontage kit — 7 setups: market exchange, deliveries, craft sales, neighbourhood life, civic announcements, urban transport, access to the quay.
Missing: narrow brick-paved street ground; tiling closed frontage band; modest house/shopfront variants; ground-level doors, shuttered windows and stoops. Reuse canal house, warehouse, bridge, handcart and wheelbarrow. All facades face camera; no compulsory side street or baked canyon perspective. Preserve regional and wealth differences; not every building an ornate merchant mansion.

3) Workshop and shop kit — 5 setups: weighing goods, measuring cloth, retail sale, packing, apprentice work.
Missing: counter, balance with weights, shelves, period measuring tools and plain workbench. Builds on room kit; existing sacks, cloth, jars, crates and barrels become stock. Keep specialist tools tied to a named trade.

4) Warehouse interior kit — 4 setups: stock inspection, cargo tally, packing, loading preparation.
Missing: heavy timber back wall band, appropriate brick floor tile, closed loading door, storage rack and plain cargo platform. Reuse existing warehouse exterior, sacks, crane, barrels and crates. Room floor and furniture are not universal substitutes for industrial storage.

TASMAN-1642 — voyage and encounter settings

1) Shipboard working interior kit — 6 setups: chart discussion, navigation instruction, log keeping, food storage, ration distribution, below-deck conditions.
Missing: timber bulkhead band, plain plank floor ground, low overhead-beam screen if visible, closed hatch/door, restrained table, bench and storage. Use one selected early seventeenth-century Dutch ship reconstruction. Existing exterior deck remains for exterior scenes; interior floor needs its own restrained lighting. Ship form and furnishings must fit Heemskerck rather than a later naval frigate. Cross-bundle charts and telescope need palette-matched production variants before inclusion.

2) Shore landing and observation kit — 5 setups: landing, shore survey, freshwater search, gathering timber, watching the coast from land.
Missing: beach ground, non-tiling landward coastal backdrop, low shore-break band, coastal vegetation, rock outcrop and stream-mouth pieces. Existing Mohua coast and sea assets support seaward views. Avoid Dutch dune vegetation pasted into Aotearoa. Never use sideways camera rotation to change facing; author a separate front-facing scene.

3) Deck work and crew-life kit — 4 setups: sail handling, lookout duty, sounding, deck maintenance.
Missing: rigging/sail screens, capstan or windlass appropriate to the ship reference, rope coils, sounding line and deck work tools. Reuse mast, rail, bulwark, deck and sea. Preserve sightlines through rigging; no full ship cut-out placed on its own deck.

4) Mohua shore-community kit — 3 setups: coastal livelihood, shore-side preparation, encounter context viewed from land.
Missing: location- and date-specific shore backdrop, compatible settlement structures, storage and work objects. Requires a selected local historical reference and Māori review of specific structures and motifs before production. Existing crewed waka is a fixed composition; it does not supply a general building vocabulary or replace separate characters. Do not fill gaps with generic Polynesian buildings.

NEAR-EAST-NEOLITHIC — chronology must be split

Inventory combines broadly Neolithic material with Uruk city material. Choose region and date before production. Uruk urban scenes belong to a later urban horizon; do not quietly stage them as an early Neolithic village. Existing mudbrick objects also need this compatibility check.

1) Domestic courtyard and room kit — 7 setups: grinding grain, preparing food, household storage, pottery use, household craft, courtyard exchange, daily shelter.
Missing: region-specific mudbrick/plastered wall band, packed-earth ground, closed entrance and niche pieces, hearth, mats, low work furniture and bins. Existing bowls, animals and tools supply dressing only after date check. Roof access and door form vary by settlement; no universal “Neolithic house” or assumed glass window.

2) Village lane and household cluster kit — 6 setups: water carrying, neighbour exchange, animal movement, construction, outdoor processing, movement between homes.
Missing: settlement-appropriate lane ground, low mudbrick boundary band, house variants, closed gate, plaster/brick repair pieces. Avoid mixing freestanding huts, dense roof-access housing and monumental urban architecture without a chosen site.

3) Cultivation and water-edge kit — 5 setups: sowing, field work, harvest, threshing, riverside collection.
Missing: silt/field ground, low crop/stubble bands, bank strip, small storage and processing pieces. Existing river, barley, palms and papyrus need regional ecological checks. Irrigation channels belong only in dated settings that support them; do not imply every early farming village had an organised canal system.

4) Uruk urban work kit — 4 setups: storage, workshop production, exchange, administration.
Missing: explicitly later-period urban wall/frontage band, courtyard floor, loading/storage fixtures and dated administrative props. Use Uruk backdrop only here, after relabelling chronology. This is a separate branch, not a completion kit for an unspecified Neolithic village.

2. FIRST BATCH: PRODUCTION AND INTEGRATION

batch1.json contains only nl-prehistory and nl-golden-age, with matching era identifiers. 12 sheets total: 5 woodland + 7 room. Woodland: floor, backdrop, two depth bands, three tree cut-outs, four undergrowth cut-outs. Room: wall, floor, window, door, fireplace, three work/storage furniture pieces, three seats.

Forest screens leave intentional gaps; distant backdrop closes the horizon. Keep near trunks out of the central teaching area. Complete tree crowns may extend above frame naturally. Never shrink mature trees into bushes to fit the viewport.

Wall overlays solve the current flat-layer constraint: window contains its own painted outside suggestion; door stays closed; fireplace has opaque firebox. Each covers unbroken wall beneath it. No alpha window can reveal a view through an opaque wall. Openable portals and connected rooms remain later work.

Place wall and wall fixtures at the same 7 m plane, fixtures drawn in front through existing layer order. Shared plane prevents detached fixtures during lateral camera movement. Window bottom about 0.9 m above floor; door and fireplace start at floor. Fit fixtures within wall height. Table and furniture stand at 3.5 m, not 22 m: full-size room furniture would become unreadable at landscape distances.

Room wall must cover entire upper viewport, horizontally and vertically, throughout allowed camera move/zoom. Floor must meet its baseline. Verify actual camera field of view; constrain interior framing to the opaque wall rectangle. Sky and backdrop remain assigned but hidden. Do not invent a second ceiling or extra ground to patch a framing failure. Any future upper wall/ceiling extension requires an explicit asset.

Ground repeats must show no seam or baked perspective. Bands must repeat without conspicuous duplicate landmarks. Match feet/root baselines, preserve real dimensions, and inspect occlusion and legibility with existing character scale. These are acceptance criteria; no images or runtime scenes have been generated or tested by this planning task.

3. THREE COMPLETE EXAMPLE SCENES

A. Woodland resources beside a small clearing — nl-prehistory, c. 3400 BCE
Teaching focus: relationship between woodland, fuel, tools and early farming. Bright diffuse daylight; no implied fixed season-specific harvest.
Sky: ONE existing nl-prehistory fair-weather daylight sky painting.
Ground: ONE trb-forest-floor-01, repeating across stage floor.
Backdrop: ONE trb-forest-backdrop-01 at bg 70 m, non-tiling; frame within panorama edges.
Layers, far to near:
- Mid 22 m: trb-woodland-screen-01 band, repeated laterally; low-contrast woodland enclosure with central gap.
- Mid 22 m: existing TRB house, glimpsed toward right-hand clearing through that gap; foreground woodland conceals its base transition. Exclude LBK longhouse.
- Fg 7 m: trb-woodland-oak-01 left; trb-woodland-birch-cluster-01 right; both full physical scale, crowns allowed above frame.
- Fg 7 m: trb-hazel-screen-01 band used in short lateral run at left margin; do not span entire teaching area.
- Near 3.5 m: trb-fern-clump-01 and trb-woodland-herbs-01 at margins; existing fallen log to right.
- Near 3.5 m: existing basket and flint axe, independently readable beside log. Existing separate teacher-selected characters occupy central gap.
No cloud layer needed. Camera level, central clearing view, tightly limited lateral move. Forest floor remains the sole ground; no grass patch painted as a second floor.

B. Household work around a table — nl-golden-age, c. 1640
Teaching focus: shared domestic space, material culture and household work. Unlit hearth, diffuse window daylight.
Sky: ONE existing nl-golden-age summer sky painting, hidden by wall.
Ground: ONE ga-terracotta-floor-01, repeated at its 2 x 2 m footprint.
Backdrop: ONE existing nl-golden-age city backdrop at bg 70 m, hidden by wall.
Layers, far to near:
- Fg 7 m: ga-room-wall-01 band, tiling across full frame, opaque from floor to above checked viewport.
- Fg 7 m, drawn over wall: ga-leaded-window-01 left, bottom 0.9 m; ga-closed-oak-door-01 right, floor-aligned; ga-hooded-fireplace-01 centre-right, floor-aligned. Keep fixtures disjoint.
- Fg 7 m: ga-oak-cupboard-01 at left-of-centre, away from window.
- Near 3.5 m: ga-trestle-table-01 centre-left; ga-rush-seat-chair-01 and ga-wooden-stool-01 beside table, not floating behind it.

No outdoor strips visible through glass: window's opaque inset supplies its own view. Camera level; fixed framing or small tested zoom only. No side walls required.
