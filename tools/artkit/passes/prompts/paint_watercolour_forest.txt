Paint this colour sketch (an underpainting of flat shapes) into a soft hand-painted watercolour illustration for an educational history book: transparent washes on white paper, light and airy, with the paper glowing through. Keep the composition, perspective, every trunk, fern and the path exactly where they are, and paint all the detail yourself in ONE consistent brush language: each tree is one painted object, its trunk and foliage made of the same kind of strokes. No photographic texture anywhere, no separate leaf cards.
Organic things get almost no lines: foliage, grass, moss and ferns are soft loose washes and wet-in-wet blooms of muted sap green, olive, moss and pale yellow-green, with only a few delicate brush marks for fronds and twigs. Tree trunks in warm grey-brown washes; only their outer edge may have a fine, broken brown pencil line. The forest floor green and mossy, scattered with small twigs and bark; the path soft earthy brown. Light falls in pale golden patches and gentle rays; shadows are soft transparent cool green-grey and blue-green, never black.
Restrained, gentle palette like a classic illustrated history book; generous pale areas. Not anime, not gouache, no opaque flat colour, no cel shading, no black ink, no hatching, no photorealism, no people, no text, no new objects.
