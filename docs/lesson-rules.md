# The rules of a History Portal lesson

Bart, 2026-10-17: "it has to have at least 1 image every sentence. it should feel snappy. we can get away
with backgrounds and moving foregrounds, change the assets. change camera position." These rules are the bar
for every lesson, written, generated or composed. A lesson that breaks a MUST is not ready, whoever made it.

## 1. Pace: one image per sentence, at least

- **MUST:** every narrated sentence gets its own image. Two sentences never share one frame.
- **MUST:** a shot lasts 3 to 8 seconds, never more than 10. Long sentence? Split it, or give it two shots.
- **MUST:** something moves in every shot: a slow camera move (pan, push-in, pull-out) and life in the world
  (water, smoke, dust, flags, birds, crowds, light). A frozen frame is a mistake.
- It must feel **snappy**: cut on the beat of the narration, like a good comic read aloud.

## 2. Many scenes, many images per scene

- A **scene** is one place and one beat, 15 to 40 seconds: **3 to 6 sentences, so 3 to 6+ shots**.
- A 15 to 20 minute lesson has **30 to 50 scenes** and **120 to 200 shots**.
- A scene can be one comic page of 2 to 4 panels, a sequence of full-frame shots, or both.

## 3. How we get that many images (and keep it affordable)

A new **shot** doesn't need a new painting. Each of these counts as a new image:
1. **Change the camera position** on the same background: wide, then medium, then close on a detail.
   Counts when the framing clearly changes (a new subject in focus, or at least about 25% more or less zoom).
   **Zoom in and use close-ups a lot** (Bart): a face reacting, hands on a rope, a coin, a ticket, a crack in
   the stone. Every scene has at least one close-up. A typical scene runs wide → medium → close-up → wide.
   Paint backgrounds at a resolution that survives a close-up (at least 2× the stage width).
2. **Move the foreground:** a parallax layer, a character, a cart, a boat, crossing or arriving.
3. **Change the assets:** swap or add figures and props on a reused background (a crowd appears, a worker
   picks up a tool, the sky turns to evening, fire, rain).
4. **A new background** for a new place or time.
- Build a scene from **one painted background plus cut-out foreground layers** (figures, props, smoke),
  never one flat painting per shot. The same background can carry a whole scene.
- Backgrounds and figures are made once and reused: LEGO logic (memory comic-look-painted-projection.md,
  one-standard-body-wardrobe.md).

## 4. Words

- **One sentence per shot, 8 to 18 words.** Cut every word the picture already says.
- Poetic: rhythm, images, the senses; not a list of facts. No EdTech phrases (see CLAUDE.md's voice rules).
- A question is followed by a **real pause** (a separate line with a delay) so the class can think.
- **Only the narrator speaks.** Characters get short silent text balloons (memory comic-balloons.md).
- Facts only from the sources; omit if unsure. No invented named people; women with agency, never invented.

## 5. People, not buildings

- Give the class **2 to 4 characters to follow** (real roles from the sources: a worker, a child, a trader,
  a sailor), with faces, hands at work and reactions. The story is about what happens to them.

## 6. The class does something every 2 to 3 minutes

- At least one every 2 to 3 minutes: a power-meter moment (skill story-gamification), a prediction ("what
  do you think happens next?"), a choice, a team game, a quiz question. Never a meter for violence.
- The first 15 seconds hook: an image and a question, never an introduction.
- End on something to wonder about, not a summary.

## 7. Look

- History lines: ink line plus watercolour (the Dante comic is the reference), drawn, never photos or photo
  crops, never blurred, no depth fade, hard shadows (memory feedback-comic-drawn-look.md).
- Inspect every image at 100%: hands, feet, faces, anachronisms, no text, nothing modern.

## 8. The check before anyone calls a lesson ready

Deliver these two tables, made from playing the WHOLE lesson muted in the real browser:

1. **Shot log:** every sentence → its shot (background, camera, foreground change) and its seconds.
   FAIL if any sentence has no shot of its own, or any shot is over 10 s.
2. **Per minute:** what changes on screen, and what the class does.
   FAIL if any minute is "a picture and a voice", or 3 minutes pass without the class doing something.

Plus the counts (scenes, shots, characters, interactions) and a contact sheet of every image. Then ask the
Feynman seat (boardroom council-feynman): "Would a class of 12-year-olds stay with this, and what will they
remember?" Its answer goes into the report unedited.
